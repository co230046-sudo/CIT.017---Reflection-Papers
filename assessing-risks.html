Assessing Risks to Protect Information Assets

David John J. De Jesus · 4th Year BSIT · August 8, 2026
  • Information Assets
  • Threats and Vulnerabilities
  • Risk Prioritization
  • Risk Mitigation
❖

💡 Tip: click any paragraph to highlight it

Risk is a constant presence in our everyday lives. At face value, it is simply the possibility of harm or an adverse event occurring. However, in the context of information management, I learned that its impact on organizations like AdZU is substantial. I realized that effective management is not about eliminating risk entirely, but rather about maintaining awareness and knowing how to handle these vulnerabilities, regardless of whether their potential impact is high or low. Risk is ultimately an inevitable part of operating an institution, which makes understanding and managing it a necessity.

During our first activity introducing the concept of risk assessment, I realized that an organization's primary vulnerabilities are rooted in its information assets. These encompass critical data such as personal records, bank account details, and other sensitive information that could be compromised. Identifying these specific assets is crucial because it allows the organization to make informed decisions on how to minimize exposure. In the activity, we learned how to organize these assets by considering not just the data itself, but also the nature of the organization and the specific applications it utilizes. From my understanding, a comprehensive risk assessment must also account for the software, hardware, and server infrastructure that supports the entire operation.

After identifying the potential threats, the actual assessment is conducted through various methods. In that same activity, we developed a detailed presentation outlining this process. One of the most important aspects I learned is the necessity of prioritization. Because it is highly impractical and costly to address every single vulnerability, it is essential to evaluate risks by establishing criteria and ranking them from most to least crucial based on the organization's context. Once these priorities are set, specific controls and measures must be implemented to mitigate the risks. Ultimately, risk assessment focuses on addressing potential gaps before they can cause substantial damage. Analyzing information assets requires a thorough consideration of the organization’s operations, allowing us to make careful decisions rather than just hoping for the best. I believe that consistent risk assessment creates a much more secure environment, as it is always better to proactively protect information assets than to simply react once a problem arises.

— D.J. De Jesus
